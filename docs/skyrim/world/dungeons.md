## 1. What counts as a dungeon, and how these rows were built

**Scope: `Skyrim.esm`.** §3 covers the DLC at zone level; `regions.md` §4 covers them at region level.

**Definition used:** a `LCTN` carrying `LocTypeDungeon` (0130DB) **or** `LocTypeClearable` (0F5E80).
That yields **226 locations**. `LocTypeDungeon` alone gives 202 and `LocTypeClearable` alone 199; the
union catches exterior dragon lairs and the Civil War camps, which are clearable-but-not-dungeon and
dungeon-but-not-clearable respectively. `[verified]`

**The join, for anyone re-deriving it:**

```
LCTN  ──ParentLocation chain──►  the LCTN carrying LocTypeHold (016771)     → hold
LCTN.Keywords                                                              → type + tileset
ECZN.Location ──reverse lookup──►  LCTN                                    → zone, MinLevel, MaxLevel
LCTN.LocationRefTypeReferencesStatic[LocationRefType == Boss (0130F7)].Ref  → placed boss ref
  └─ that PlacedNpc's Base + LevelModifier                                 → boss record + difficulty
LCTN.LocationRefTypeReferencesStatic[… == LocationWordWall (0E7520)]        → WW column
```

Two gotchas that cost time and are worth recording:

- **`grep -rl` over `reference/` times out** (already in CLAUDE.md). Building a
  `FormID → EditorID → record type` index from *filenames* once, then joining against it, turns a
  multi-minute search into a lookup. `[verified]`
- **Placed records are nested at different indentations** in `Cells/` (top-level list, `- Mutagen…`)
  versus `Worldspaces/` (nested one level deeper, `  - Mutagen…`), and a `PlacedNpc` block itself
  contains inner `- MutagenObjectType: ScriptObjectProperty` entries. A parser that anchors on
  column 0, or that treats any nested `MutagenObjectType` as a record boundary, silently drops
  records — it cost two rebuilds here (5,169 → 9,806 → **10,504** placed NPCs). Track the opening
  line's indentation and only close the record at the same or lesser indent. `[verified]`

### How a dungeon boss is actually chosen

The `Boss` reference resolves to a `PlacedNpc` whose `Base` is almost always a `Lvl*` **wrapper NPC**
— a level-1 record with `TemplateFlags: Stats` whose `Template` points at a leveled list:

| Wrapper NPC | `Template` → | which is |
|---|---|---|
| `LvlGiant` | `030529` | `LCharGiant` (LVLN) |
| `lvlDragon` | `05EACF` | `LCharDragonAny` (LVLN) |
| `LvlSilverhandBoss` | `03DF17` | a bandit-boss LVLN |
| `LvlWarlockConjurerBoss` | `0E0FFD` | a warlock-boss LVLN |
| `LvlDraugrAmbushBossMale` | `0B7988` | a draugr-boss LVLN |

**So the dungeon record does not name its boss.** It names a wrapper that defers to a leveled list,
and the only per-dungeon input is the placed ref's `LevelModifier`. `[verified]` That is exactly the
seam Ehlnofey needs: **change the wrapper's template per dungeon and you have hand-set bosses without
touching a single NPC record.**

The exceptions are the hand-authored uniques — `JyrikGauldurson`, `ValsVeran`, `Linwe`,
`dunAnsilvundLuahAlSkaven`, `dunSkuldafnNahkriin`, `dunRagnOtar`, `dunValthumeHevnoraak`, `Ianusu`,
`dunGeirmundSigdis`, `dunCrackedTuskGhunzul`, `DA03Wizard` — which are placed directly and already
have fixed levels. `[verified]`

| **BanditCamp** | **6** | 6 ×28, 2 ×1, 5 ×1, 8 ×1 |
| **DraugrCrypt** | **6** | 6 ×20, 12 ×1, 14 ×1 |
| **VampireLair** | **6** | 6 ×8 |
| **WerewolfLair** | **6** | 6 ×2 |
| **Shipwreck** | **6** | 6 ×6, 12 ×1 |

That is a clean, near-monotonic ladder: **2 → 6 → 8 → 14 → 16 → 18 → 24**, with the type keyword
predicting the zone level correctly in ~85% of cases. Vanilla already encodes "difficulty is a
property of the *kind* of place". `[verified]`

| the Reach | 31 | 2 ×1, 6 ×4, 8 ×4, 14 ×11, 16 ×2, 18 ×2, 24 ×2 |
| the Rift | 30 | 2 ×5, 6 ×13, 8 ×1, 12 ×1, 14 ×1, 16 ×1, 18 ×2, 24 ×1 |
| Eastmarch | 25 | 2 ×4, 5 ×1, 6 ×11, 8 ×2, 10 ×1, 14 ×2, 16 ×1, 18 ×1 |

| Whiterun | 21 | 2 ×4, 6 ×8, 8 ×3, 12 ×1, 14 ×1, 18 ×1, 24 ×1 |
| Hjaalmarch | 18 | 2 ×4, 6 ×7, 10 ×1, 16 ×1, 18 ×1, 24 ×1 |
| Haafingar | 18 | 2 ×1, 6 ×8, 8 ×4, 12 ×1, 14 ×1, 24 ×1 |

Every hold spans nearly the full range. There is **no vanilla danger gradient across Skyrim** — a
level-24 Dragon Priest lair sits in eight of the nine holds, and a level-2 giant camp sits in seven.

**Consequence for Ehlnofey.** Bone 2 says *danger is legible*. Vanilla makes it legible by **type**
(you can see it's a Dwemer ruin) but not by **place** (nothing tells you the Reach is harder than
Falkreath, because it isn't). So `regions.md` and `design/difficulty-map.md` are inventing a gradient,
not recovering one — and any regional scheme has to fight the existing type-based signal rather than
build on it. That is a real design tension to resolve on paper, not mid-build.

The Reach is the closest thing to an exception (11 of its 31 dungeons at level 14, driven by Forsworn
and hagraven density) and is the natural pilot region if Ehlnofey wants to test a regional tier before
committing to all nine holds. `[verified]`

### Other structural facts

- **Only 4 dungeons have a level ceiling.** `BleakFallsBarrowZone` 6–20, `EmbershardMineZone` 6–10,
  `HaltedStreamCampZone` 6–10, `WhiteRiverWatchZone` 6–10. (The fifth capped zone in the game,
  `WinterholdCollegeMiddenZone` 6–30, is not a dungeon.) All four are **early-game tutorial dungeons
  on the Riverwood/Whiterun path** — Bethesda capped exactly the places a new player might reach
  before they could survive them, and nowhere else. `[verified]` That is the vanilla precedent for
  what Ehlnofey wants to do to all 280 zones.
- **28 dungeons have no encounter zone at all**: 18 Civil War military camps (radiant, by design),
  **8 of the 10 exterior dragon lairs**, plus Orphan Rock, Bard's Leap Summit, Karthspire and
  Volskygge. An unzoned dungeon has no level floor and no `NeverResets` protection — its difficulty
  is entirely the leveled list plus the placed `LevelModifier`. `[verified]`
- **41 of 226 dungeons are `NeverResets`** — Labyrinthian, Korvanjund, Irkngthand, Kilkreath,
  Northwatch Keep, Potema's Catacombs, Nightcaller Temple, Ustengrav, Frostmere Crypt, Mzulft,
  Cracked Tusk Keep, Glenmoril Coven and 29 others. These are mostly quest-critical. A deleveling
  pass must not change respawn behaviour on them by accident. `[verified]`
- **31 dungeons hold a word wall.** They correlate strongly with the top of the type ladder (dragon
  lairs, Dragon Priest lairs, the deeper Nordic ruins) and are the natural anchor points for a
  progression-gated tier in `progression.md`. `[verified]`
- **`LevelModifier` census across all placed actors** (cells + worldspaces, 10,504 `PlacedNpc`):
  Easy 3,520 / Medium 1,393 / Hard 554 / VeryHard 218 / none 4,819. So **54% carry one**, and the
  distribution is heavily skewed to `Easy`. `[verified]` (`enemy-taxonomy.md` §5 also gives the
  interior-cell-only subset, 2,524 of 4,452 — same field, narrower scope. Note that counting this
  field with grep over-reports: 215 `PlacedObject` **markers** carry it inertly.)
  Note the pattern in the tables below: **boss refs are overwhelmingly `VeryHard`**, which is how
  vanilla makes a boss feel like a boss without giving it a different record.

### Bleak Falls Barrow is parented to Falkreath

`BleakFallsBarrowLocation` (018EE9) has `ParentLocation: 01676F` = `FalkreathHoldLocation`, despite
sitting above Riverwood on the map. `[verified]` Several other Whiterun-adjacent sites are likewise
parented south, which is why Whiterun shows only 21 dungeons and Falkreath 34.

**Do not silently "fix" this.** The `ParentLocation` graph is what the game's own systems read, so a
region-based rule built on hold membership will behave the way this table says, not the way the map
looks. If `design/difficulty-map.md` wants map-accurate holds it must say so explicitly and define
its own mapping. One location (`Ironbind Barrow`) has no hold in its parent chain at all and is listed
under *Unparented* below.

## 3. DLC dungeons

Counted the same way as §1 (`LocTypeDungeon` ∪ `LocTypeClearable`), the DLC add **7** new dungeons
(Dawnguard) and **38** (Dragonborn); Hearthfire adds **none**. Dawnguard's other 13 dungeon-tagged
locations are *overrides* of vanilla ones. Full region-level treatment, including the places that
carry no keywords and so appear in no count here, is `regions.md` §4.

Dragonborn is the closest thing to a first-party proof that a fixed-band world works, and it is worth

The only capped zone in any DLC is `DLC2DremoraShopZone` at `MaxLevel: 99` — effectively uncapped.
`[verified]`

## 5. What this gives Phase 3

1. **The tier ladder is already in the data.** `2 / 6 / 8 / 14 / 16 / 18 / 24` by dungeon type, and
   the taxonomy's archetype gates land on the same spacing. `design/tiers.md` should start by
   *ratifying* this ladder, not inventing one — it is the cheapest possible route to a fixed world
   that still feels like Skyrim.
2. **`difficulty-map.md` has 226 rows and they are already filled in.** Assigning tiers is editing a
   column in the tables above, not researching 226 dungeons.
3. **Boss control is a wrapper swap.** Retarget the `Lvl*` wrapper NPC's `Template` per dungeon and
   every boss becomes hand-set, with no NPC record edits. This is the single most promising
   implementation lever found so far and belongs in `implementation-strategy.md`.
4. **The regional gradient does not exist and must be authored.** That is a bone-2 design problem
   (legibility), not a data-extraction problem. The Reach is the most coherent starting region.
5. **28 dungeons have no zone**, so a zone-based design leaves them uncovered — most importantly
   8 of the 10 dragon lairs. Either create zones for them or accept a second mechanism.
6. **Solstheim is the worked example.** Before designing bands from scratch, read what Dragonborn did
   with 6/10/20/25/30/40 across 57 zones.

4. What governs the **28 unzoned dungeons** and the exterior dragon lairs in practice? `[unverified]`
5. Do the 41 `NeverResets` zones behave differently under a `MaxLevel` change, given a
   never-resetting cell keeps whatever it rolled on first entry? `[unverified]` — this decides
   whether Ehlnofey's changes apply to an existing save at all.
